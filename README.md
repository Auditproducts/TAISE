# About TAISE

**TAISE** stands for **Trusted AI Safety Expert**.

The TAISE certificate is developed by the **Cloud Security Alliance (CSA)** in partnership with **Northeastern University**. It is designed to provide professionals with knowledge and practical understanding needed to support the **safe, secure, and responsible development and deployment of AI systems**.

TAISE takes a broad view of AI safety and trust. It is not limited to AI model development. The curriculum covers areas such as:

* Artificial intelligence fundamentals
* Generative AI
* Generative AI architectures
* AI use cases
* Ethics and responsible AI
* Transparency and explainability
* AI model lifecycle
* AI threats
* AI governance
* Risk management
* Compliance
* AI security and safety
* Cloud security
* Data security
* Privacy
* Continuous learning and adaptation

The overall objective is to understand how AI systems can be designed, developed, deployed, governed, secured, monitored, and improved responsibly across their lifecycle.

### What does an 80% passing score mean?

The examination contains **60 questions** and requires an **80% score**.

That means a candidate needs approximately:

**48 correct answers out of 60**

to achieve an 80% score.

However, candidates should not prepare with the mindset of simply reaching the minimum passing score. A stronger preparation strategy is to aim for a comfortable margin above the passing threshold.

# TAISE Exam Structure

TAISE currently consists of **10 modules**, covering the major areas of trustworthy AI, safety, security, governance, and responsible AI adoption.

The modules are:

### Module 1 — Introduction to AI

This module establishes the foundations of artificial intelligence.

Topics include fundamental AI concepts, AI terminology, different approaches to AI, machine learning concepts, AI capabilities, limitations, and the broader development of AI.

This module is particularly important because later TAISE modules build upon many of these foundational concepts.

---

### Module 2 — Generative AI Architecture and Design

This module focuses on the architecture and design principles behind generative AI systems.

It introduces concepts related to generative models, architectures, components, data, models, prompts, and supporting infrastructure.

Understanding how generative AI systems work is important for later discussions involving security, risk, governance, and responsible deployment.

---

### Module 3 — Generative AI Use Cases and Applications

This module examines how generative AI can be applied to real-world scenarios.

The focus moves from understanding the technology to understanding how it is used in organizations and applications.

Candidates should consider not only the benefits of generative AI but also the potential risks and limitations associated with different use cases.

---

### Module 4 — Ethics, Transparency, and Explainability in AI

This module addresses responsible and trustworthy AI.

Key areas include concepts such as:

* AI ethics
* Bias
* Fairness
* Transparency
* Explainability
* Accountability
* Responsible AI
* Trust
* Human oversight

This area is particularly important when evaluating AI systems that make or influence decisions affecting people.

---

### Module 5 — AI Model Lifecycle and Threat Taxonomy

This module examines the lifecycle of AI models and the threats that can arise throughout that lifecycle.

Understanding the lifecycle helps organizations identify where security, safety, privacy, governance, and risk controls need to be applied.

Candidates should understand that AI security is not something that should be added only after a model has been deployed.

---

### Module 6 — Model Governance, Risk Management, and Compliance

This module focuses on organizational controls around AI.

Important areas include:

* AI governance
* Risk management
* Compliance
* Policies
* Standards
* Controls
* Regulatory considerations
* Accountability
* Risk assessment

Candidates should understand how organizations can establish processes to manage AI risks while supporting responsible AI adoption.

---

### Module 7 — Introduction to AI Security and Safety

This module introduces the security and safety considerations associated with AI systems.

It is important to distinguish between:

**AI safety**

and

**AI security**

while also understanding where the two areas overlap.

The module helps learners understand potential AI-specific threats and the need for controls throughout the AI lifecycle.

---

### Module 8 — Cloud and AI Security

This module connects AI security with cloud security.

Modern AI systems frequently depend on cloud infrastructure, APIs, storage, compute resources, identity systems, networking, and other cloud services.

Therefore, protecting an AI system also requires understanding the security of the underlying cloud environment.

---

### Module 9 — Data Security and Privacy in AI Systems

Data is fundamental to many AI systems.

This module focuses on protecting data and maintaining privacy throughout the AI lifecycle.

Important concepts include:

* Data security
* Data privacy
* Data governance
* Data minimization
* Protection of sensitive information
* Appropriate data handling
* Privacy considerations throughout the AI lifecycle

---

### Module 10 — Continuous Learning and Adaptation

AI systems do not necessarily remain static.

Models, data, applications, threats, regulations, and business requirements can change over time.

This module focuses on the importance of continuous monitoring, learning, adaptation, and improvement.

The goal is to ensure that AI systems remain trustworthy and appropriately governed as their environment changes.

# Stage 4 — Question Bank

The question bank is intended for **self-assessment and practice**.

Use it only after studying the relevant concepts.

A good approach is:

1. Study the official material.
2. Review the supplementary notes.
3. Attempt questions without looking at answers.
4. Identify incorrect answers.
5. Return to the official material.
6. Understand why the answer is correct.
7. Reattempt the question later.

The objective is **not to memorize question-and-answer pairs**.

Instead, use questions to test whether you understand the underlying concept.

# Scenario-Based Thinking

TAISE preparation should not be limited to memorizing definitions.

Many AI safety, governance, ethics, risk, and security problems are easier to solve when you understand the underlying principle.

When you encounter a scenario, ask:

### 1. What is the AI system doing?

Understand the use case first.

### 2. What could go wrong?

Identify possible risks.

### 3. Who or what could be affected?

Consider users, organizations, data, systems, and other stakeholders.

### 4. What type of risk is involved?

For example:

* Bias
* Privacy
* Security
* Safety
* Governance
* Compliance
* Transparency
* Explainability
* Data protection

### 5. What control or mitigation would address the risk?

Think about appropriate governance, technical, organizational, or procedural controls.

### 6. What principle is the question testing?

This is often more useful than simply remembering a particular sentence.

---

# How to Use the Question Bank Effectively

Avoid treating the question bank as a collection of answers to memorize.

Instead, classify every question into one of these categories:

* **I know it**
* **I partially understand it**
* **I guessed correctly**
* **I got it wrong**
* **I need to revisit the official material**

Questions that you guessed correctly should not automatically be considered mastered.

A correct answer based on guessing is still a knowledge gap.

# Open-Book Does Not Mean No Preparation Is Required

The TAISE exam is described by CSA as an **online, open-book, unproctored examination**.

However, open-book should not be interpreted as "the material can be searched for every question."

The exam has a **120-minute limit for 60 questions**.

That gives an average of approximately:

**2 minutes per question**

Therefore, strong conceptual familiarity is important.

The purpose of studying the material beforehand is to make it possible to recognize concepts quickly and use the available reference material efficiently when necessary.

# Time Management During the Exam

A practical approach is:

### First Pass

Answer questions for which you are confident.

### Second Pass

Return to questions requiring more thought.

### Reference Material

Use the official material when you need to verify a concept, terminology, or distinction.

### Final Review

Reserve time to review unanswered or uncertain questions.

Do not spend too much time on one difficult question at the expense of easier questions later in the exam.

Understand concepts such as fairness, ethics, transparency, explainability, and accountability.

## AI Risk

Understand how AI risks arise and how they can be assessed and mitigated.

Understand how organizations establish policies, controls, responsibilities, and processes around AI.

Understand the relationship between AI workloads and cloud infrastructure.

## Data Security

Understand why data protection is fundamental to trustworthy AI.

Understand how AI systems can create privacy concerns and how privacy should be considered throughout the lifecycle.

Understand why AI systems require ongoing evaluation and improvement.
